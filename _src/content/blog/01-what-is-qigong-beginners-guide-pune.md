If you have ever watched a group of people moving slowly and calmly in a park at sunrise and wondered what they were doing, there is a good chance it was qigong. Pronounced "chee-gong", it is one of the oldest wellness practices in the world, and it is quietly growing in India as more people look for a gentle way to manage stress, stiffness and low energy.

This guide explains what qigong is, what it can and cannot do, and what to expect when you join a qigong class in Pune or online with Alloha Qi Space.

## What does "qigong" mean?

The word has two parts. "Qi" is usually translated as life energy or vital breath. "Gong" means skill developed through steady practice. Put together, qigong is the skill of working with your own energy through movement, breath and attention.

Indians often find the idea familiar. Qi is close to what our own traditions call prana, and many qigong principles echo pranayama and dhyana. The style is different, though: qigong is soft, flowing and done mostly standing, with no extreme stretches.

## How qigong works

A typical qigong practice brings together three things:

- **Slow, repeated movements** that gently open the joints, spine and shoulders.
- **Deep, relaxed breathing** from the belly, coordinated with each movement.
- **Calm focus**, often with simple visualisation, which settles a busy mind.

Because nothing is forced, qigong suits people who find gym workouts too intense or who cannot get down on a yoga mat comfortably.

## Benefits people commonly report

Research on qigong is still growing, so it is best seen as a complement to medical care, not a replacement. With that said, studies and health institutions describe qigong as a helpful practice for:

- Reducing stress and calming the nervous system
- Improving balance, posture and flexibility
- Easing stiffness from long hours at a desk
- Better sleep and a more settled mood
- Gentle activity for older adults and people recovering from illness

Most beginners notice the calming effect first, often within the first session.

## Is qigong safe for beginners?

Yes. Qigong is low-impact and can be adapted for almost any age or fitness level, including seated practice. As with any exercise, go gently, stop if something hurts, and speak to your doctor first if you have a heart condition, are pregnant, or are recovering from surgery.

## What happens in a beginner qigong class in Pune?

At Alloha Qi Space, a beginner session usually runs for about an hour and follows a simple flow:

- **Arrival and settling** – a few minutes of standing or seated breathing.
- **Warm-up** – loosening the neck, shoulders, wrists, hips and knees.
- **Main form** – a short sequence of flowing movements, taught step by step.
- **Stillness** – standing meditation or guided relaxation.
- **Closing** – time to notice how you feel and ask questions.

Wear loose, comfortable clothing and flat shoes, or practise barefoot. You do not need any equipment.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to learn qigong?

You can learn a simple routine in one or two sessions. Real depth comes from regular practice over a few months.

### How often should I practise?

Ten to twenty minutes a day is more useful than one long session a week.

### Is qigong religious?

No. Qigong has roots in Chinese philosophy and medicine, but it is practised today as a mind-body exercise by people of all faiths.

### Do I need to be flexible?

Not at all. Qigong works within your current range of movement.
