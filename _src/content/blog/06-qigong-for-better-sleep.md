Late nights, screens, work stress and busy minds have made poor sleep a common problem in Indian cities. Many people lie in bed feeling tired but wired, with thoughts racing long after the lights go off.

Qigong offers a gentle, natural way to help the body wind down. This short evening routine takes about ten minutes and can be done in your bedroom before you sleep.

## Why we struggle to sleep

Sleep comes most easily when the nervous system shifts from "alert" mode into "rest" mode. Stress, caffeine, screens and late work keep us in alert mode. Slow breathing and gentle movement help switch the body back toward rest. Health institutions such as the Cleveland Clinic list sleep among the areas where qigong is being studied as a complementary practice.

## A 10-minute qigong routine for sleep

Dim the lights, put your phone away and wear something comfortable.

### 1. Gentle shaking (1 minute)

Stand and gently bounce from the knees, letting your arms and shoulders hang loose. This releases tension held from the day.

### 2. Rolling the shoulders and neck (1 minute)

Roll your shoulders slowly backward 5 times, then forward 5 times. Gently tilt your head side to side.

### 3. Sinking the energy (2 minutes)

Stand with knees soft. Breathing in, slowly raise your arms in front of you to chest height. Breathing out, press your palms slowly downward toward the floor, imagining tension draining out through your feet. Repeat 8 times. This is one of the most calming movements in qigong.

### 4. Rubbing the hands and face (1 minute)

Rub your palms together until warm. Place them gently over your eyes and rest for three breaths. Then lightly stroke your face from forehead to chin.

### 5. Lying-down breathing (5 minutes)

Lie on your back in bed. Place one hand on your belly. Breathe in slowly for a count of four, letting the belly rise, and breathe out for a count of six. A longer out-breath helps the body relax. Continue until you feel drowsy.

## Sleep habits that support your practice

- Stop screens 30 to 60 minutes before bed if you can.
- Keep caffeine to the morning.
- Try to sleep and wake at similar times each day.
- Keep your room cool, dark and quiet.

## When to see a doctor

If poor sleep lasts for weeks, or you snore heavily, wake up gasping, or feel exhausted during the day, please speak to a doctor. Qigong can support good sleep, but some sleep problems need medical care.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I do this routine?

About 15 to 30 minutes before bed works well for most people.

### Can qigong help with insomnia?

It may help you relax and fall asleep more easily. Long-lasting insomnia should be discussed with a doctor.

### Should I do energising qigong at night?

No. Keep evening practice slow and calming, and save energising movements for the morning.
