For anyone raised in India, breathing practice usually means pranayama, the breath techniques of yoga. So when people start qigong, they often ask how its breathing compares. Is it the same thing with a different name, or something new?

The answer is a bit of both. Qigong and pranayama share a deep respect for the breath, but they approach it in different ways.

## Prana and qi: two words, one idea

Both traditions describe a life energy that flows through the body and is closely linked to breath. In yoga it is called prana, and in Chinese tradition it is called qi. Both believe that calm, steady breathing helps this energy flow smoothly and supports health and a clear mind.

## Key similarities

- Both use slow, deep, conscious breathing.
- Both emphasise breathing through the nose.
- Both link breath to calming the mind.
- Both see breath practice as a path to meditation.

## Key differences

- **Movement:** Pranayama is usually practised seated and still. Qigong breathing is often combined with slow movement, so the breath and body move together.
- **Intensity:** Some pranayama techniques, such as kapalbhati and bhastrika, are vigorous. Qigong breathing stays soft, quiet and natural.
- **Breath retention:** Many pranayama practices include holding the breath (kumbhaka). Most beginner qigong avoids holding the breath.
- **Focus point:** Qigong often directs attention to the lower belly, called the dantian, about three finger-widths below the navel.

## A simple qigong breath to try

### Dantian breathing

- Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees soft, or sit upright in a chair.
- Place both hands, one over the other, on your lower belly.
- Breathe in slowly through your nose, letting the belly gently expand.
- Breathe out slowly and let the belly soften.
- Keep the breath smooth, quiet and unforced.

Continue for 3 to 5 minutes. If you already practise pranayama, notice how this feels softer and more relaxed.

## Combining qigong with your pranayama practice

Many of our students at Alloha Qi Space in Pune practise both. A common approach is to do a few minutes of qigong movement first to relax the body, then sit for pranayama. Others use qigong breathing as a calming finish after strong pranayama. If you have high blood pressure, heart conditions or are pregnant, avoid forceful breathing and breath retention, and consult your doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is qigong breathing safe for beginners?

Yes. It is gentle and does not involve forceful breathing or breath-holding.

### Can I replace pranayama with qigong breathing?

You do not need to. They complement each other well.

### How long should I practise each day?

Start with five minutes a day and build up gradually.
