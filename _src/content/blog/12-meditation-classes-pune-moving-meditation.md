"I tried meditation, but I just can't sit still." If that sounds like you, you are not alone. Many people in Pune sign up for meditation classes, then give up because sitting with closed eyes makes their mind louder, not quieter.

The good news is that sitting still is not the only way to meditate. Qigong, often called moving meditation, offers a gentler door into the same calm.

## Why sitting meditation feels hard

When the mind is busy, asking it to go quiet can feel like a battle. The body gets restless, the back aches and thoughts multiply. For beginners, especially those who are stressed, overthinking or sitting all day for work, this can be frustrating.

## What is moving meditation?

Moving meditation uses slow, deliberate movement as the anchor for attention. Instead of focusing only on your breath while sitting, you focus on the feeling of your arms rising, your weight shifting and your breath matching each motion. The body is busy enough to settle the restlessness, while the mind slowly becomes calm.

## How qigong works as meditation

- **Movement gives the mind something to hold.** It is easier to follow a slow arm movement than an abstract idea of "emptiness".
- **Breath and movement sync.** This naturally slows and deepens the breath.
- **Standing stillness comes later.** After moving, most people find it much easier to stand or sit quietly for a few minutes.

## Who moving meditation suits

- Beginners who struggle with sitting meditation
- Overthinkers and people with racing minds
- People with back or knee discomfort when sitting on the floor
- Busy professionals who want calm and focus in a short time
- Experienced meditators who want to add body awareness

## Try a 5-minute moving meditation

- Stand with feet hip-width apart and knees soft.
- Breathing in, slowly float your arms up in front of you to shoulder height.
- Breathing out, slowly let them sink back down.
- Keep your attention on the feeling in your palms and arms.
- When your mind wanders, gently return to the movement.

Repeat for three minutes, then stand still with your hands on your lower belly for two minutes and simply notice your breath.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is qigong a real form of meditation?

Yes. Qigong is widely described as moving meditation and often includes standing or seated meditation too.

### How long should a beginner meditate?

Five to ten minutes a day is a great start.

### Do I need to sit cross-legged?

No. Qigong meditation is done standing or seated in a chair.
