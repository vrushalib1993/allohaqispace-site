In India, exam season can feel overwhelming. Board exams, JEE, NEET, CET and college finals bring long study hours, pressure from all sides and very little rest. Pune, with its many colleges and coaching centres, sees this every year.

A little stress can help you focus, but too much makes it harder to remember, sleep and perform. Qigong offers short, simple practices that help students calm down and concentrate, without taking much time away from studying.

## How stress affects studying

When stress builds up, breathing becomes shallow, the body tenses and the mind races. This makes it harder to concentrate and recall information. Calming the body is often the fastest way to calm the mind.

## Practice 1: Calm breathing before studying (2 minutes)

- Sit upright with both feet flat on the floor.
- Place your hands on your lower belly.
- Breathe in slowly through your nose for a count of four.
- Breathe out slowly for a count of six.

Repeat ten times before you open your books. A longer out-breath helps the body relax.

## Practice 2: Study break reset (3 minutes)

Every 45 to 60 minutes, stand up and:

- Shake out your hands, arms and legs for 30 seconds.
- Raise your arms overhead as you breathe in, and lower them as you breathe out, 6 times.
- Roll your shoulders back 5 times.

This refreshes circulation and attention after long periods of sitting.

## Practice 3: Focus palms (1 minute)

Rub your palms together briskly until warm, then gently cup them over your closed eyes. Rest for three slow breaths. This relaxes tired eyes after screen time and reading.

## Practice 4: Before the exam (1 minute)

While waiting outside the exam hall, stand with your feet firmly on the ground and take five slow belly breaths. Feel the weight of your body in your feet. This simple grounding practice helps settle nervousness.

## Healthy exam-season habits

- Sleep seven to eight hours, especially the night before an exam.
- Take short breaks instead of studying for hours without stopping.
- Drink enough water and eat regular meals.
- Limit late-night caffeine and phone scrolling.
- Talk to someone you trust when the pressure feels too much.

## For parents

Students are more likely to keep a practice going if the family does it too. Try a five-minute breathing session together each evening during exam season.

## When to ask for help

If a student feels persistently hopeless, cannot sleep or eat, or feels unable to cope, please reach out to a school counsellor, doctor or mental health professional. Breathing practices support wellbeing but are not a substitute for professional help.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do these practices take?

Each takes one to three minutes, so they fit easily into a study schedule.

### Can breathing really help in an exam?

Slow breathing helps calm nervousness, which can make it easier to think clearly.

### Are these practices suitable for younger children?

Yes, the breathing and stretching practices are gentle and safe for school students.
