Do you wake up tired, need tea or coffee to get going and crash again by afternoon? Feeling low on energy has become one of the most common complaints of modern life, especially for people juggling work, family and long commutes.

In qigong, energy, or qi, is the central focus of practice. Its gentle exercises are designed to help you feel more awake and alive without the jolt of caffeine or the exhaustion of an intense workout.

## Why we feel drained

Tiredness can come from many places: poor sleep, stress, sitting for long hours, shallow breathing, irregular meals, dehydration and too much screen time. Sometimes it is also a sign of a medical issue such as anaemia, thyroid problems or vitamin deficiencies, which are common in India. If you feel exhausted for weeks without a clear reason, please see a doctor and get checked.

## How qigong helps restore energy

- **Gentle movement** improves circulation without draining you.
- **Deep breathing** brings in more oxygen and calms stress.
- **Relaxation** releases tension that quietly uses up energy.
- **Focus** clears mental fog and scattered thinking.

Many students describe feeling both calm and awake after practice, a combination that is hard to get from coffee.

## Four quick energy practices

### 1. Body tapping (1 minute)

With loose fists, gently tap along your arms, shoulders, chest, lower back and legs. This wakes up the body and is great first thing in the morning or during an afternoon slump.

### 2. Bouncing (1 minute)

Stand with soft knees and gently bounce up and down, letting your arms and shoulders hang loose. This shakes off sluggishness quickly.

### 3. Lifting the sky (2 minutes)

Interlace your fingers in front of your belly. Breathe in as you lift your hands overhead with palms facing up, stretching gently. Breathe out as you float your arms down. Repeat 8 times.

### 4. Gathering energy (2 minutes)

Breathing in, sweep your arms out to the sides and up overhead, as if gathering energy from around you. Breathing out, bring your palms down in front of your body to rest on your lower belly. Repeat 6 times, then rest with your hands on your belly for a few breaths.

## Daily habits that support your energy

- Keep a regular sleep schedule.
- Drink water through the day, especially in Indian summers.
- Eat regular, balanced meals instead of skipping breakfast.
- Get some morning sunlight.
- Take a short movement break every hour.

## Best times to practise

Morning practice sets your energy for the day. A quick two-minute routine after lunch helps beat the afternoon slump. Avoid energising practices close to bedtime; use calming ones instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can qigong replace coffee?

Many people find a short practice gives them a gentler lift, which helps them rely less on caffeine.

### How soon will I feel more energetic?

Many people feel more awake right after a session. Lasting change comes with regular practice.

### What if I am always tired despite practising?

Please see a doctor. Ongoing fatigue can have medical causes that need proper testing.
