Qigong for Back and Neck Pain: Gentle Relief for Desk Workers

Sitting all day? These gentle qigong exercises help ease stiffness in the back, neck and shoulders. Simple routines for desk workers in Pune and India.

Back and neck stiffness has become one of the most common complaints among working people in India. Hours in front of a laptop, long commutes and looking down at phones all add up. By evening, many people feel tight between the shoulder blades, sore in the lower back or stiff in the neck.

Qigong is a gentle way to keep the spine moving and relieve that everyday stiffness. It does not replace a doctor or physiotherapist, but it can be a valuable daily habit alongside their advice.

Why desk work causes stiffness

When we sit for long periods, the hips tighten, the upper back rounds forward and the head drifts ahead of the shoulders. Muscles that should be relaxed start working overtime, while others switch off. Over time this leads to aches, poor posture and fatigue.

How qigong helps

Qigong uses slow, repeated movement to gently mobilise the joints and spine. Because the movements are soft and coordinated with breathing, they help muscles relax rather than tense. Health sources such as HealthLinkBC note that qigong may improve posture, balance and flexibility.

Five gentle qigong exercises for the back and neck

1

Turning the headneck release

Stand or sit tall. Breathing out, slowly turn your head to look over your right shoulder. Breathing in, return to centre. Repeat to the left. Do 5 rounds each side and keep the movement small and comfortable.

2

Waist turningspinal twist

Stand with feet wider than hips, knees soft. Let your arms hang loose. Gently turn your waist left and right, allowing your arms to swing and lightly tap your body. This loosens the lower back and hips.

3

Spinal wave

Stand with feet hip-width apart. Slowly bend your knees and let the movement roll up through your hips, lower back, upper back and neck, like a soft wave. Repeat 6 to 8 times. Keep it small at first.

4

Pushing the mountains

Bring your hands to your chest, palms forward. Breathing out, slowly push your palms forward as if pressing a wall, letting your shoulder blades spread. Breathing in, draw your hands back. This opens the upper back.

5

Holding the ball

Stand with knees slightly bent and arms rounded in front of your chest as if hugging a large ball. Relax your shoulders and breathe slowly for 2 to 3 minutes. This builds gentle strength in the legs and back while teaching the body to relax under effort.

Tips for safe practice

  • Move within a pain-free range. Mild stretch is fine; sharp pain is not.
  • Keep your knees soft, never locked.
  • Breathe slowly and never hold your breath.
  • If you have a slipped disc, sciatica or a recent injury, check with your doctor or physiotherapist first.

Make it a habit

Try this routine once in the morning and once after work. Over a few weeks, many people notice they sit taller and feel less tight by evening.

Frequently asked questions

Can qigong cure back pain?

Qigong is not a cure, but it can ease stiffness and support recovery alongside medical advice.

How long before I feel a difference?

Some relief can come after one session. Lasting improvement usually takes a few weeks of regular practice.

Can I do this if I have a slipped disc?

Speak to your doctor or physiotherapist first. A teacher can then adapt movements for you.

A note on health

Qigong is a gentle complement to medical care, not a replacement for it. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or are recovering from surgery, please check with your doctor before starting a new practice.
