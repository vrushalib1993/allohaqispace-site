Qigong vs Yoga: Which Practice Is Right for You?

Qigong or yoga? Compare movement, breathing, intensity and benefits to choose the right mind-body practice for you. A clear guide for India.

In India, almost everyone has tried yoga at some point. So when people hear about qigong, the first question is usually: "How is it different from yoga, and which one should I do?"

The honest answer is that both are excellent, and many people enjoy doing both. But they feel quite different in the body, and one may suit your needs better right now. Here is a clear comparison.

Where they come from

Yoga comes from India and is thousands of years old, with roots in philosophy, breath control and meditation. Qigong comes from China and grew out of traditional Chinese medicine, martial arts and Taoist meditation. Both see the body, breath and mind as connected, and both work with a concept of life energy: prana in yoga, qi in qigong.

How they feel

  • Yoga often involves holding postures, stretching deeply and moving between the floor, sitting and standing.
  • Qigong is mostly done standing, with slow, continuous, flowing movements and very little holding or deep stretching.

A simple way to picture it: yoga often asks you to go into a shape, while qigong asks you to keep moving gently, like water.

Side-by-side comparison

  • Intensity: Yoga ranges from gentle to very demanding. Qigong stays gentle and low-impact.
  • Floor work: Yoga uses a mat and many floor postures. Qigong needs no mat and can be done standing or seated.
  • Flexibility needed: Some yoga styles need good flexibility. Qigong works within your current range.
  • Breathing: Yoga uses many pranayama techniques, some quite strong. Qigong breathing is soft, slow and tied to movement.
  • Best for: Yoga is great for strength, flexibility and discipline. Qigong is excellent for calm, balance, joint comfort and energy.

When qigong may suit you better

  • You have stiff knees, back pain or find it hard to get up and down from the floor.
  • You are over 55 and want a safe practice for balance.
  • You feel stressed, wired or tired, and want something calming rather than challenging.
  • You sit at a desk all day and want a short routine you can do in office clothes.

When yoga may suit you better

  • You want to build strength and deep flexibility.
  • You enjoy a structured, physically challenging class.
  • You already love yoga and want to go deeper in that tradition.

Can you do both?

Yes, and they complement each other beautifully. Many of our students in Pune do yoga two or three times a week and use qigong on other days, or as a short morning or evening routine. Qigong is also a lovely way to cool down after a strong yoga session.

Frequently asked questions

Is qigong easier than yoga?

Physically, qigong is usually gentler. Mentally, it still asks for focus and patience.

Is qigong good for weight loss?

Qigong is not a calorie-burning workout. It can support healthy habits by reducing stress, which often drives emotional eating.

Which is better for anxiety?

Both can help. Many people find qigong's slow, flowing movement especially calming when the mind is racing.

A note on health

Qigong is a gentle complement to medical care, not a replacement for it. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or are recovering from surgery, please check with your doctor before starting a new practice.
